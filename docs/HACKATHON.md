# Hackathon project brief

## Problem statement

During a disaster or personal emergency, people may have limited internet access, need to share their location quickly, and face uncertainty about what to do next. Emergency contacts, offline maps, first-aid guidance, and ways to signal for help can be spread across separate tools, adding friction when time matters.

## Project overview

Guardian is an Android emergency-support app designed to help people take practical steps during disasters and personal-safety emergencies, including when mobile data is unavailable. It brings SOS messaging to saved contacts, an offline India map, on-device emergency guidance, and rescue tools together in one app.

## How it helps

Guardian helps users act on several immediate needs from one screen flow: contact trusted people, communicate their location by SMS where cellular service is available, orient themselves on an offline map, consult on-device guidance, and signal to people nearby. Nearby relay can extend an alert between phones running Guardian when the devices can connect.

Guardian complements official emergency response. For immediate danger in India, users can call 112; Guardian does not claim to notify or dispatch police, fire, or ambulance responders directly.

## Social impact

Guardian is designed for people who may be isolated by a disaster, at risk while travelling, or helping someone who is injured. Combining preparation, communication, navigation, and guidance in a single tool can make the first steps easier to find and use.

### Example

Someone is stranded during flooding and mobile data is unavailable. They use Guardian’s offline map to check their GPS position, send their coordinates and an SOS by SMS to saved contacts, consult offline first-aid guidance, and use a torch signal to attract nearby attention. They can call 112 for official emergency response.

## Differentiation

Guardian’s project idea is the combination: offline mapping, on-device AI guidance, contact-based SOS messaging, and hands-on rescue tools in one Android app. This story focuses on complementing India’s 112 system with practical actions the user can take, rather than claiming to replace emergency services.

## Suggested short pitch

> Guardian is an offline-first Android emergency toolkit. It combines an India offline map, on-device Gemma guidance, SOS messages with location for saved contacts, and rescue signals in one app—so people can take practical steps even when mobile data is unavailable, while keeping 112 within reach.

## Suggested live demo

1. Show Guardian’s SOS screen and a saved test contact.
2. With prior consent from a test contact, submit one demo SOS and show the app’s SMS submission status. Android submission does not confirm carrier delivery.
3. Disable Wi-Fi and mobile data, then open the offline map and show the GPS marker.
4. Ask AID a relevant emergency question and show the local response.
5. Activate Morse audio or Torch SOS and stop it using the in-app control.
6. Explain relay using two Guardian devices if available; otherwise show the relay status and describe the device-to-device requirement.

Keep the presentation focused on a working end-to-end scenario. Use only a test contact who has agreed in advance, tell them before sending, and include “DEMO” in the incident details. Do not call 112 or send an SOS to an unsuspecting person during the presentation. Narrate what is happening while the app is on screen.
